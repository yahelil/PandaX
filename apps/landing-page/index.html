Who this is for

If you build connected equipment and place it on the EU market, the Cyber Resilience Act asks you to show your work. This is for the people who have to produce that.

You are probably a fit if

  • You manufacture connected industrial or electrical equipment and sell it into the EU.
  • You ship often enough that the same evidence work comes round again.
  • Someone has already asked you for a technical file, an SBOM, or a reporting timeline.
  • Today that answer is assembled by hand, by a few people, close to the release date.

What it gives each of you

  • Product security and compliance

    The evidence for your next release, organised and reviewable — with your test lab in the room rather than waiting on the outcome.

  • Engineering and quality leadership

    One evidence process that repeats across every product family, instead of rebuilding the story from scratch on every release.

  • Test labs and consultancies

    A structured evidence pack your reviewers can reuse. We do not compete with your assessment work, and we never charge on its outcome.

The same work, every release

One finding sets off four jobs. The next release sets off the same four, from the top.

  1. 01

    Break down the release

    Catalogue every component and library in the release.

  2. 02

    Assess the exposure

    Trace each vulnerability to the affected product and version.

  3. 03

    Prove the fix shipped

    Gather the remediation evidence and show which release carried it.

  4. 04

    Submit the evidence

    Enter the required information by hand through the CRA reporting process.

then all four again, next release

24h early warning. 72h notification. The countdown begins with manufacturer awareness.

What the Act asks you to keep

Not a scan report. A file that shows what you shipped, what was wrong with it, and what you did — kept for ten years.

  1. 11 Sep 2026 Vulnerability-handling requirements apply In effect
  2. 11 Dec 2027 Full CRA application
  • Companies must maintain technical files — cybersecurity risk assessments, software architecture designs and test reports — available to authorities for at least 10 years.
  • Penalties can reach €15 million or 2.5% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher.

European Commission Digital Strategy; Regulation (EU) 2024/2847, Articles 71 and 73.

The clock starts when you know

Both deadlines run from the moment you become aware — not from the moment you have a fix.

  1. 24 hours

    Early warning

    Alerting for an actively exploited vulnerability or severe incident.

  2. 72 hours

    Notification

    Affected product information, measures taken and user protection steps.

  3. 14 days

    Actively exploited vulnerability

    Final report, once a corrective or mitigating measure becomes available.

  4. 1 month

    Severe incident

    Final report, after the 72-hour notification.

Reports are submitted through the CRA Single Reporting Platform.

Who we are

PandaX is an independent European workspace for product security evidence.

Scanners find things. Managed services do the work on your behalf. Neither of them owns the document at the end — the technical file comes out as a by-product of somebody else's tool, and gets assembled by hand again every time a release ships.

We built PandaX to own that part. The record is the product: every component, finding and decision tied to the release that actually shipped, in a form a reviewer can read and a form you can take somewhere else.

We stay neutral about scanners, the export is portable, and we never charge on the outcome of a conformity assessment. PandaX produces documentation. It does not issue opinions, certificates or verdicts.
