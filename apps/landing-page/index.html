What a reviewer asks for

  • The risk assessment and how it was resolved

  • A timeline that still holds up two years later

The evidence has to exist before the legal review starts.

Four stages.

  1. 1

    Extraction

    A build or firmware package goes in. PandaX unpacks it and records the digest of the file it read.

  2. 2

    Detection

    The inventory is matched against public advisory sources. Sources that failed are shown as failed, not as clean.

  3. 3

    Evidence

    Each risk decision is recorded as it is made, with its rationale, its author and its date.

  4. 4

    Sealed release file

    The result is sealed to the release and timestamped, so a reviewer can check that nothing changed afterwards.

No source code required. The build never leaves the customer's control.

Questions worth asking.

How is this different from a firmware scanner?

A scanner produces findings. PandaX produces the file a reviewer asks for: the inventory tied to the shipped image, the decision made about each finding, who made it and when, and the reasoning. A scan is an input to that file, not a substitute for it.

Do you need our source code?

No. PandaX works from a build or firmware package. Source code is never required and the build never leaves your control.

Where does our data sit, and who can read it?

The proof of concept runs locally, on your machine. The hosted workspace is being built and will be EU-hosted; the access model is not settled yet, and we would rather say so than describe something that does not exist.

What happens to our evidence if we stop being a customer?

The export is an open format and it is yours. It is designed to be read without PandaX installed, which is also what makes it useful to a reviewer.

Does this make us CRA-compliant?

No. PandaX produces the documentation and records the process. Conformity is assessed by a body, not by a tool. Any vendor that tells you otherwise is selling you a problem.

What exists today, and what is still being built?

Today: ingest, component inventory, findings from public sources, recorded decisions, the Article 14 timeline, document generation and a sealed, independently verifiable export. Being built: the hosted multi-user workspace, scheduled advisory monitoring and broader firmware format coverage.
